# Claimant Slice 2 Threat And Control Matrix

Last updated: 2026-07-31 (Asia/Dubai)

Status: Draft for threat-model review. No claimant runtime is authorized.

## Rating And Acceptance Rules

- `Critical`: could cause unauthorized plaintext access, irreversible release, private-key compromise, cross-tenant evidence access, or systematic authority bypass.
- `High`: could enable account/case takeover, sensitive evidence disclosure, reviewer abuse, durable denial of access, or material legal/privacy harm.
- `Medium`: creates bounded integrity, availability, enumeration, or operational harm without independently authorizing release.
- Critical threats require preventive controls, negative tests, monitoring, incident containment and independent review before the relevant runtime slice.
- Risk acceptance must name the owner, rationale, expiry date and compensating controls. Silence is not acceptance.

## Residual Risks Requiring Owner Acceptance

- A claimant who legitimately decrypts information may copy it; Sanduqkin cannot guarantee recall.
- Hardware-backed keys reduce extraction risk but do not protect an already-compromised unlocked device from every authorized-use attack.
- Multi-device custody reduces loss risk but increases key/grant lifecycle complexity and attack surface.
- Human review reduces automated error but introduces insider, inconsistency, staffing and delay risks.
- Evidence quarantine intentionally brings sensitive claimant PII into Sanduqkin's processing boundary and changes the privacy/incident profile.
- A single-region primary and third-party providers create outage and jurisdictional dependencies that controls can reduce but not eliminate.

## Threat-Model Exit Gate

Before Slice 3, security records:

1. every Slice 3 threat has an owner and planned test;
2. no Critical Slice 3 risk is accepted without independent review and explicit owner approval;
3. legal/privacy confirms the planned data and evidence boundary;
4. physical custody evidence supports the selected platform scope;
5. claimant capabilities and all kill switches remain off; and
6. the decision-register versions referenced by tests and designs are immutable.
